🔎 Three patterns from history

  • Recession sets the depth.Bear markets without a recession fell a bit over 20% on average and bottomed within months; those with a recession fell about 40% on average and often dragged on for a year or two.
  • Bottoms almost always come from a “policy pivot.”In 10 of 13 cases, the bottom was triggered by Fed easing, a government rescue or a policy retreat (a tariff pause), not by waiting for good news.
  • When the bottom arrives, the news is usually still bad.At the bottoms of March 2009, March 2020 and October 2022, economic data and earnings were still getting worse.

🧭 When is it better to start buying?

No one can call the exact bottom; below is a framework built from historical patterns
Two scenarios
Scenario A: AI correction, no recession (more likely)Like 2018, 2022, 2025: a 15–25% drop before bottoming, with the key being the Fed stopping hikes or a policy overhang being resolved. The bottom may come with news between the 12/10 FOMC and the new Congress convening in January.
Scenario B: AI bubble bursts + recessionLike 2000–2002: capex slashed, private credit blowups, rising unemployment; indexes could fall 30–50% in several legs over a year or two. Then you need more tranches spread over a longer time.
